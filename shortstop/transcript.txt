Shortstop might be the hardest position to play on a baseball field.
And it’s not just because you need to have good hands.
First, shortstops have to cover an enormous amount of ground.
They’re responsible for balls hit up the middle, deep in the hole, and everything in between.
Then there’s the hops.
A shortstop has to react to hard ground balls, bad hops, and sharply hit balls with almost no time to adjust.
But fielding the ball is only half the job.
Shortstops often have to throw across their body, make off-balance throws, or release the ball while moving away from first base.
And on top of all that, they have to communicate with the second baseman, cover bases, and make split-second decisions depending on the situation.
So the next time you see a shortstop make a routine-looking ground-ball play, remember…
There’s a lot more going on than just catching the ball.
