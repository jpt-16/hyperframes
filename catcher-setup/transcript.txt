Did you know a catcher’s positioning can change depending on which side of the plate the hitter stands?

When a right-handed hitter steps into the box, the umpire and catcher have a particular viewing angle.

But when a left-handed hitter steps up, that angle changes.

The catcher has to adjust to the hitter’s position, the pitch location, and the umpire’s line of sight.

Why does this matter?

Because catchers want to give the umpire the clearest possible look at the pitch while receiving it smoothly.

On borderline pitches, good framing can also help the umpire recognize the pitch as a strike.

At the highest level, catching isn’t just about stopping the baseball.

It’s about understanding everything happening around home plate.
