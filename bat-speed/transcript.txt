Bat speed isn’t created by the arms alone.
It’s the result of how the entire body works together.
It starts with how the hitter interacts with the ground.
Ground reaction forces help support and generate the movements that contribute to the swing.
As the swing develops, the pelvis and torso rotate in a coordinated sequence.
Their timing and rotational acceleration help create the conditions for the upper body to accelerate the bat.
But the lower body doesn’t create bat speed by itself.
The arms and hands play a major role in accelerating the barrel and controlling its path through the hitting zone.
Bat speed comes from the coordination of all these movements.
It’s not one muscle, one body part, or one coaching cue.
It’s the entire movement working together.
