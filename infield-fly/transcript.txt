This is one of baseball's weirdest rules.
Bases loaded, less than two outs. The batter hits a pop-up, and the infielder lets it drop on purpose.
The runners take off, the defense gets easy outs… except they don't.
That's the infield fly rule.
When a fair fly ball can be caught by an infielder with ordinary effort, with runners on first and second—or the bases loaded—and fewer than two outs, the umpire can call the batter out immediately.
Why? To stop the defense from dropping an easy pop-up and turning it into a cheap double play.
And here's the twist: the runners can still advance at their own risk.
Baseball has a rule for a play that looks too easy.
