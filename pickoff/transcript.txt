One tiny step can cost a team an ENTIRE extra base.
Watch what happens here. The pitcher keeps his pivot foot on the rubber, steps toward first, and makes a pickoff throw. But the ball sails into the dugout.
The runner is awarded just ONE base.
Now watch the difference. Before throwing, the pitcher legally steps backward off the rubber with his pivot foot.
He then throws the ball away, and it goes out of play.
This time, the runner gets TWO bases.
It’s because once the pitcher legally disengages the rubber, the rule treats him as an infielder for that throw.
So a wild throw off the rubber is treated like a wild throw by another infielder.
Same pitcher. Same target. Different foot position.
And that one detail changes the entire base award.
