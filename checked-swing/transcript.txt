What actually counts as a swing in baseball?
You might think it's as simple as whether the bat crosses home plate, but it's not.
There is no universal rule that says the bat must cross a specific point to count as a swing.
Instead, umpires use their judgment to determine whether the batter attempted to strike the ball.
If the home plate umpire initially calls a pitch a ball, the defensive team can appeal the checked swing to the first or third base umpire.
That umpire then makes the final decision.
And here's something interesting: if the batter checks his swing but still puts the ball in fair territory, it's a live ball.
So next time you see a checked swing call, remember: it's not just about where the bat ends up. It's about whether the batter attempted to swing.
