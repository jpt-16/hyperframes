A hit-and-run is one of baseball's biggest calculated risks.
With a runner on first, the runner takes off as soon as the pitcher delivers the ball.
But here's the catch: the hitter is expected to swing and make contact.
Why?
Because the second baseman or shortstop has to cover second base, opening up a hole in the infield.
If the hitter puts the ball through that gap, the runner can advance an extra base.
It can also make a double play much harder because the runner is already moving toward second.
But if the hitter swings and misses, the runner could be an easy target for the catcher.
One pitch can turn a smart strategy into a costly mistake.
That's the hit-and-run.
