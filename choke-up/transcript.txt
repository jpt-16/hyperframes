Is choking up on the bat actually worth it? The answer depends on the hitter and the situation. When a hitter chokes up, they move their hands higher on the handle, shortening the effective length of the bat. This can make the bat feel easier to control and help a hitter get the barrel through the zone more consistently. It can be especially useful when facing high velocity, protecting with two strikes, or trying to make consistent contact. But there’s a tradeoff. Choking up reduces some of the leverage and reach you get from holding the bat at the very bottom. So why don’t all hitters do it? Because hitting isn’t just about making contact. It’s about finding the right balance between bat speed, control, power, and timing. Some hitters choke up with two strikes. Others do it on nearly every pitch. And some never need to change their grip at all. The bottom line? Choking up can be worth it if it helps you make better contact without sacrificing too much power. It’s not about swinging harder. It’s about controlling the barrel.
