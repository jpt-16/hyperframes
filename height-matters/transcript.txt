The 2026 World Series is challenging one of baseball's biggest assumptions.
The Brewers and Rays have some of the shortest lineups in recent World Series history.
In fact, the Rays' starting lineup has just one player taller than six feet.
So does height actually matter in baseball?
Being taller can give players longer levers, and pitchers may benefit from a higher release point.
But being shorter can come with advantages too.
A shorter hitter may have a smaller strike zone, while some shorter infielders can benefit from getting lower to the ground.
And the research is interesting.
A 2023 study examining 319 first-round MLB draft picks found no relationship between players' physical measurements in high school and their eventual MLB performance, measured by WAR.
The takeaway?
Height can influence how a player moves and plays, but it doesn't determine how good they become.
The Brewers and Rays are proving that talent doesn't come in one size.
