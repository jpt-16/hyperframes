Have you ever wondered how a hitter can know what pitch is coming before the pitcher even throws it? It’s called tipping pitches, and sometimes pitchers give away their biggest secret without even realizing it. A pitcher might use a slightly different glove position, hand movement, or setup depending on which pitch they’re throwing. Sometimes, the difference is even more subtle, like a change in delivery timing or how they handle the ball. But here’s where it gets interesting. Teams study video and look for patterns. If they notice the pitcher consistently doing something different before throwing a slider compared to a fastball, they can start recognizing the tell. And once they figure it out, that information can spread throughout the team. A runner at second base might recognize the catcher’s signs and relay information to the hitter. Coaches and teammates can also communicate through prearranged signals. The challenge is making sure the pitcher doesn’t realize they’ve been figured out. Because if a hitter knows what’s coming, even a fraction of a second earlier, it can completely change the at-bat. And that’s why pitchers work so hard to make every pitch look exactly the same. In baseball, sometimes the biggest advantage isn’t having a better pitch. It’s knowing which pitch is coming.
