The hardest app to build isn’t necessarily the one with the most complicated code.
It’s the one that needs two completely different groups of people to succeed.
And this is a problem companies like Uber and DoorDash had to solve.
Think about it.
Customers won’t use Uber if there are no drivers.
And drivers won’t join Uber if there are no customers.
It’s the same problem with DoorDash.
No restaurants means no customers, and no customers means no reason for restaurants to join.
So how did they solve it?
They didn’t try to take over the entire country on day one.
They focused on one city, built up enough supply and demand to make the platform useful, proved the model worked, and then expanded into new markets.
Now think about an app like DetailDash.
It’s essentially DoorDash for car detailing.
It connects people who want their cars detailed with detailers who want more customers.
But building the app is only half the battle.
You need enough detailers in an area to attract customers, and enough customers to make joining worthwhile for detailers.
That’s why the smartest way to build a marketplace isn’t to launch everywhere.
It’s to dominate one market first.
Then expand.
