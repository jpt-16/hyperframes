When a pitcher starts struggling, what should a catcher say during a mound visit? The first thing? Don’t make it worse. A catcher shouldn’t walk to the mound and start listing everything the pitcher is doing wrong. Instead, they need to figure out what’s happening. Is the pitcher rushing? Losing confidence? Missing his spots? Or just struggling to get ahead? Sometimes, the best thing to say is simple: “Take a breath. Slow everything down. We’ve got this.” If the pitcher is struggling with command, the catcher can remind him to focus on one target instead of trying to be perfect. If he’s getting frustrated, bring his attention back to the next pitch. Not the last one. And if the pitcher is tipping his pitches or changing his mechanics, that’s when the catcher can communicate a specific adjustment. But here’s the biggest thing: A good catcher doesn’t try to fix everything in one conversation. They give their pitcher confidence, a clear plan, and a reason to trust the next pitch. Because sometimes, the most important part of a mound visit isn’t what the catcher says. It’s how they get their pitcher to believe in himself again.
